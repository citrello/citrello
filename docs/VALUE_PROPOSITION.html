Citrello is the free, open-source reference manager for the AI era. It lets Claude and ChatGPT cite only real, checked papers, checks and fixes the references in any paper, and works alongside Word, LaTeX, Zotero, Mendeley and EndNote instead of replacing them.

The AI problem, and why it's Citrello's moment

Researchers and students now draft, summarise and edit with ChatGPT and Claude. References are where this breaks most visibly: an invented citation looks real until someone checks it. Universities are writing AI policies, journals are retracting papers with fabricated references, and supervisors are checking theses by hand.

Mendeley users

Researchers who rely on Mendeley, often because their institution or supervisor does

What they struggle with

  • Mendeley retired its classic Desktop app in 2022; many people found the replacement (Reference Manager and the Mendeley Cite add-in) slower or missing features they relied on.
  • An online account is required, and some researchers are uneasy about a publisher (Elsevier) holding their library.
  • Duplicates and wrong metadata accumulate over years.
  • Papers mix old Mendeley Desktop citations, new Mendeley Cite citations and typed ones.
  • No built-in way for ChatGPT or Claude to use the library.

How Citrello helps

  • Imports a Mendeley library (account or exported file), skipping duplicates and checking every reference.
  • Reads papers made with Mendeley Desktop and with Mendeley Cite, finds the problems, and fixes them into live citations.
  • Its Word citations use Mendeley's own format, so co-authors on Mendeley can keep working on the same file.
  • Library on your computer, free, open; AI assistants connected.

First "aha" moment

Dropping a Mendeley Cite thesis into the checker and seeing a duplicate and two wrong DOIs nobody had noticed.

Not yet as good as Mendeley at

Reading and annotating PDFs, shared group libraries, the Word tab on Mac. Mendeley Cite support is built from its documented format and needs testing on more real files.

Zotero users

Researchers who chose the free, open-source standard

Zotero is excellent and trusted, and these users are the hardest to win as a replacement. Citrello should present itself as the AI and checking layer next to Zotero.

What they struggle with

  • Their AI assistants can't use their Zotero library without third-party plugins or servers.
  • Good BibTeX needs an extra plugin (Better BibTeX), and LaTeX keys can change.
  • Free online storage is small; syncing large libraries costs money.
  • They can't easily check a co-author's paper made with Mendeley, EndNote or typed by hand.

How Citrello helps

  • One-click import of the Zotero library on the same computer, no account or API key; collections become projects.
  • Claude, ChatGPT and Codex use that library through the connector.
  • Checks and fixes any paper, whatever made it.
  • Checks every reference against the publisher's record, including DOIs that exist but point to a different paper.

First "aha" moment

Asking Claude "write my related-work paragraph from my Zotero collection, as a Word file in Nature style" and getting it, with live citations.

Zotero is stronger at

PDF reading and notes, group libraries, saving from thousands of websites, a mature plugin ecosystem, retraction alerts (Zotero has these too). Citrello should not compete on those.

LaTeX and Overleaf users

Mathematicians, physicists, engineers, computer scientists and many PhD students

What they struggle with

  • BibTeX copied from search engines: missing DOIs, acronyms lowercased by the style ("Lidar" for LiDAR).
  • The same paper under two keys; a key cited but missing from the .bib; entries never cited.
  • Old papers with hand-typed thebibliography lists that are painful to convert.
  • Linking Overleaf to Mendeley or Zotero needs a paid Overleaf plan (as of our last check).

How Citrello helps

  • Clean BibTeX and biblatex with stable keys, protected acronyms and escaped special characters.
  • Checks a .tex file or Overleaf project zip for missing, unused and duplicate entries and unreliable references.
  • Converts hand-typed bibliographies into a .bib and matching \cite keys.
  • Keeps a .bib file up to date automatically; AI assistants write \cite{Key}.
  • Planned: a private .bib link Overleaf refreshes, on Overleaf's free plan.
AI-first writers

Researchers and students who draft, edit and summarise with Claude, ChatGPT or Codex

"I don't trust any reference my AI gives me, so I check every one by hand."

What they struggle with

  • Invented or wrong references, and the time it takes to check each one.
  • Copying references between the chat and Word, then formatting them.
  • University AI policies and fear of misconduct questions.

How Citrello helps

  • 16 tools for the AI: search, add with checks, format, write Word documents with live citations, verify, import, BibTeX, LaTeX checks.
  • Works in Claude Desktop (one-click extension), Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT (paid plans with Developer mode) and claude.ai on the web and phone.
  • The same library in every chat, so the AI remembers your references.

First "aha" moment

The AI says "this DOI doesn't exist; I won't cite it" instead of making one up.

Limits

Web connectors (ChatGPT, claude.ai) need Citrello running on a computer. Citrello checks that a reference is real and correct, not that it supports the sentence.

Beginners

Undergraduates, master's students and first-year PhD students with no reference manager

What they struggle with

  • Typing references by hand and learning a style's rules.
  • A last-minute rush to build the bibliography before a deadline.
  • Renumbering everything after moving a paragraph.
  • Reference managers look complicated, and they don't know where to start.

How Citrello helps

  • One search box: paste a DOI, a link or a typed reference and press Enter.
  • Already typed it by hand? Fix and format turns the document into live citations in any style.
  • The free web checker needs no install: a first taste with zero commitment.
  • A clear rating for every reference teaches what a good reference looks like.
Supervisors, editors and universities

The people who check other people's references, and the institutions that buy tools

What they struggle with

  • Checking a thesis or manuscript's references by hand.
  • Detecting AI-invented references and enforcing AI policies.
  • IT approval and data-protection rules for any tool they recommend.

How Citrello helps

  • The checker gives a reference report for any thesis in a minute, as a spreadsheet.
  • Open source and local storage make IT approval easier.
  • Later, a university licence: single sign-on, managed installs, team libraries, support.

Strengths and gaps

Where Citrello is strongest

  • Timing: AI-invented references are a current, visible problem, and nobody owns the answer yet.
  • Neutral ground: works with every tool and every document, so nobody has to switch to try it.
  • A free hook with no install: the browser checker gives value in one minute.
  • Trust: open source, local library, private checking, opt-in emails only.
  • Breadth from day one: Word, LaTeX, five AI clients, Windows and Mac, imports from all major tools.
  • Built-in update channel and usage numbers: one-click updates and anonymous counts from launch.
  • Brand: a memorable name with a UK trademark application filed (6 November 2026).

Gaps and risks

  • Install warnings on Windows and Mac until code signing; they cost downloads.
  • Untested on many real files: Mendeley Cite and EndNote formats need real-world testing.
  • No PDF reader or group libraries, which long-time Zotero and Mendeley users expect.
  • Word tab is Windows-only for now.
  • One maintainer: support load and continuity.
  • Copying: the big AI companies or the existing reference managers could add similar features; speed and community are the defence.
  • ChatGPT connector needs a paid ChatGPT plan with Developer mode.

What each audience needs to know

For a new user

Citrello keeps your references in one place on your computer and makes them work in Word, LaTeX and your AI assistant. It's free.

Start in five minutes

  1. Not ready to install? Check a paper at citrello.app/check.
  2. Download Citrello and import your Zotero or Mendeley library, or paste a DOI in the search box.
  3. Connect Claude, ChatGPT or Codex in Settings.
  4. On Windows, add the Word tab, insert citations, and change the style any time.

Your library never leaves your computer. An account is optional.

For a product manager

What matters most

  • Activation: a first reference added, or a first paper checked.
  • Aha: a check that finds a real problem, or an AI-written Word file with live citations.
  • Retention: weekly active installs, connector use, repeat checks.

Available signals

Anonymous daily installs by version and system, sign-ups, feature-email opt-ins, active connector users (in the admin stats).

Priorities, in order

  1. Test with 5–10 real users and their own papers; fix what breaks.
  2. Launch around the free checker.
  3. Code signing (SignPath approved → signed Windows builds).
  4. Overleaf live .bib link.
  5. Word tab for Mac (Office add-in).
  6. Group libraries and sync (the paid tier).

For the CEO

Positioning

"The reference manager for the AI era": the layer that makes AI-assisted research trustworthy, free and open, next to the tools people already use.

Strategy

  • Wedge: the free paper checker, shareable and installable by nobody.
  • Platform: the library shared by Word, LaTeX and every AI assistant.
  • Revenue later: sync and team libraries ("Citrello Plus"), university licences, research job ads. Never selling user data.
  • Synergy: Citrello reaches researchers and the professors who act as referees, the people Refmee needs, as a separate, endorsed brand with separate consent for marketing.

Protect

Trademark (filed in the UK; US/EU decision by 6 May 2027), AGPL licence with contributor terms that allow relicensing, a published trademark policy.

For an investor

Problem and timing

Millions of researchers (UNESCO counts roughly 9 million full-time researchers, plus far more students) now write with AI, and references are where AI fails most visibly. Universities and journals are reacting now.

Product

Built and tested on Windows and Mac (version 1.0.1), with automated end-to-end tests, a live web checker, live AI connectors for five clients, accounts, usage counting and in-app updates.

Traction

Before public launch: no user numbers yet. Usage counting and accounts are in place to measure from launch day.

Business model

Free core to win adoption; paid sync and team features; university licences (single sign-on, managed installs, data agreements, support); job ads; synergy with Refmee.

Main risks

Adoption against entrenched free tools, larger players copying the idea, and a single maintainer.
